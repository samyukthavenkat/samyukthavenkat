# Corporate Credit Analysis — Volkswagen Financial Services AG

**Project:** Independent corporate credit analysis and financial modelling  
**Analysis date:** 13 October 2026  
**Basis:** FY2025 IFRS annual report, restated H2 2024 comparative and FY2023 predecessor-group context  
**Currency:** € million unless stated otherwise

## Executive credit view

The preliminary analyst view is **solid credit quality with a negative bias**. VW FS AG is the wholly owned captive finance arm of Volkswagen AG. The analysis considers earnings, capital, leverage, asset quality, funding, liquidity, parent linkage and automotive-industry risks.

The central credit question is whether earnings and capital generation can keep pace with balance-sheet growth and increasing exposure to guaranteed vehicle residual values, particularly for battery-electric vehicles (BEVs). Funding and liquidity are supported by diversified sources and regulatory liquidity buffers, while the credit profile remains closely linked to Volkswagen Group.

This is an independent educational analysis, not an external credit rating, investment recommendation, lending decision or representation of Volkswagen's views.

**Comparability caveat:** the current VW FS AG group perimeter (European operations plus VW Bank) was formed on 1 July 2024. H2 2024 covers six months only, while FY2023 represents a different global predecessor group. Headline FY2025 growth versus H2 2024 is distorted by comparing twelve months with six months. Annualising H2 2024 is indicative only and ignores seasonality and one-offs.

## Main risk assessment

1. **EV transition and residual values — High.** Guaranteed residual values rose 22% to €64.1bn. The memo identifies pressure on first-generation BEV values and €675m of lease-asset impairments in FY2025.
2. **Capital intensity — High.** CET1 declined from 18.1% to 16.3%; risk-weighted assets grew faster than equity.
3. **Parent and rating linkage — High.** The credit view is linked to Volkswagen AG through the group relationship and support arrangements described in the memo.
4. **Dealer and fleet credit — Medium.** Corporate default rate increased from 3.2% to 3.7%, while Stage 3 allowances increased by approximately 21%.
5. **Funding and interest-rate risk — Medium.** Funding is diversified, but continued portfolio growth depends on customer deposits and wholesale-market access.
6. **Geographic concentration — Medium.** The memo identifies significant dependence on Germany's contribution to operating result.

See [the risk register](analysis/risk_register.csv) for the evidence and analyst read-through.

## Debt-servicing and liquidity

The memo highlights thin earnings cover as a structural feature of a finance company, stronger cash interest cover, and external funding needs associated with portfolio investment. It identifies customer deposits, the self-liquidating receivables and lease-asset book, parent support and capital-market access as relevant repayment and liquidity considerations.

The analysis records an LCR range of 124–184% through 2025, against a 100% minimum. It also notes that liquid assets cover 57% of twelve-month wholesale maturities, rising to approximately 98% when Bundesbank-pledged ABS is included. Recheck these figures against the underlying annual report before external use.

## Further diligence questions

- What is the legal entity borrowing, and what support is legally available beyond the profit-transfer agreement and comfort letter?
- Can management provide pro-forma FY2024 and H1 2026 results to clarify the underlying earnings trend?
- What portion of guaranteed residual values relates to BEVs, by brand and vintage, and how is risk shared with dealers or the OEM?
- What is the expected CET1 trajectory under planned growth, capital actions and profit transfers?
- What are the largest Stage 3 dealer and fleet exposures, their collateral coverage and expected default trends?
- What are the size and tenor of the VW AG backup line, deposit-stability assumptions and asset encumbrance?
- How sensitive are funding costs and facility terms to a Volkswagen AG downgrade?
